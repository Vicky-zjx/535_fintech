01 / RESEARCH OBJECTIVE / A HYPOTHESIS, NOT A RESULT

Can selectivity improve the Wheel?

I hypothesize that adding trend, volatility and sudden-drop filters to a multi-asset weekly Wheel may reduce downside exposure compared with an unfiltered Wheel, although these restrictions may reduce premium income and capital utilization.

The test below establishes how much the unchanged filters alter historical entry eligibility. It does not yet establish better returns or downside protection: those require comparable option trades, costs and complete NAV histories. Course proposals can use an explicitly hypothetical $40,000 cash-only starting state; they do not establish actual account returns.

02 / THE PROCESS

From the nightly book to the next session.

The Wheel, one phase at a time

  1. Cash → short put

    Sell one OTM put and reserve strike × 100 in cash. The premium is a receipt, not the strategy’s net profit.

  2. Put assignment → shares

    If assigned, buy 100 shares at the strike. If the put expires without assignment, wait for the next weekly put-entry session.

  3. Shares → covered call

    After Sanji confirms the stock position, evaluate a call for the next session. Keep at least 100 unencumbered shares behind it.

  4. Call assignment → cash

    If called away, return to the cash phase. Otherwise retain shares and evaluate another qualifying call.

Proposals are not positions

Without an official book, course mode starts with hypothetical $40,000 cash and zero holdings. Sanji alone confirms fills, expiry and assignment. Unconfirmed proposals reserve collateral and lock their ticker; the planner never edits positions or invents fills.

No rolls, stop-losses, naked calls or borrowing. Stock losses can exceed all premiums collected.

04 / ENTRY & CONTRACT SELECTION

Explicit rules. Observable inputs.

New puts are planned only for the first actual XNYS session of the week, including holiday-adjusted Tuesdays. Calls may be planned for any next session after confirmed assignment. All signals use the latest completed session; a run during an open session is blocked.

01

Trend alignment

Close ≥ SMA50
SMA20 ≥ SMA50

SMAn = mean of the last n completed-session closes. Avoid initiating bullish exposure against a weakening trend; recoveries may be missed.

Twenty sessions react faster to recent direction; fifty provide a slower reference. Requiring both alignment and a close above that reference avoids buying a weak trend solely for its option premium. Both averages lag prices, so sharp recoveries can begin before the screen allows entry, and sideways markets can produce repeated reversals.

Illustration only: close 50, SMA20 49 and SMA50 48 pass.

02

Volatility ceiling

HV20 ≤ 45%

Sample standard deviation (ddof = 1) of 20 daily log returns × √252. Requires 21 closes. Historical volatility is not implied volatility.

The 45% ceiling limits new exposure when recent realized price variability is high. It also removes some potentially richer-premium opportunities. HV measures the past; it neither measures the option's implied volatility nor shows whether its premium is attractive relative to future realized risk. The cutoff is a research choice, not a fitted optimum.

Illustration only: HV20 of 38% passes; 46% fails.

03

Sudden-drop protection

R5 ≥ −8%

R5 = Closet / Closet−5 − 1. Avoid opening puts after sharp declines; the rule can also miss a rebound.

The five-session horizon catches a fast decline that a 50-day average may absorb only slowly. It is an entry gate, not a stop-loss on stock already assigned. The −8% boundary deliberately postpones new downside obligations during a sharp fall, at the cost of missing a possible reversal immediately afterward.

Illustration only: 50 → 46 is −8%, at the boundary.

These initial thresholds are risk-motivated, not statistically optimized. Fifty consecutive valid session closes are required. Put trend, volatility and sudden-drop filters are not automatic call-entry requirements.

Cash-secured puts

  • Actual observed OTM puts, 3–10 calendar DTE measured from the order’s for date; choose the observed expiry closest to 7 days (earlier on a tie), then test strikes. If none qualifies, skip rather than silently changing the tenor.
  • Closest absolute delta to 0.25 when the eligible expiry slice has reliable same-session Greeks. Ties go further OTM.
  • Incomplete Greeks: nearest observed strike to 97% of the closing price. This fallback is not a 25-delta estimate.

Covered calls

  • At least 100 shares, no covering call or conflicting pending order; one contract per ticker.
  • Same 3–10 DTE range and +0.25 delta target.
  • Strike must meet both 1.01 × known stock cost basis and 1.02 × latest close.
  • Without reliable Greeks, select exactly two observed strikes above ATM and verify both price floors. If this fails, skip; do not silently move the strike.

Income, assignment and short-dated risk

The −0.25 put and +0.25 call targets favor out-of-the-money contracts: the intent is to collect premium while leaving room before assignment or the call's upside cap becomes binding. Moving farther out generally sacrifices premium. Delta is a local price sensitivity; using its absolute value as an assignment-probability shortcut is only an approximation, not a guaranteed 25% frequency, especially with early exercise and changing volatility. These signs describe the option, not the short position's delta. OIC: delta.

A roughly seven-day target matches the weekly decision cycle; the 3–10 calendar-day band accommodates the observed expirations without requiring a nonexistent exact tenor. It does not mean every order expires the Friday of its entry week. Short maturity allows frequent reassessment and time decay, but near-the-money gamma can rise sharply near expiry. A small late stock move can therefore change exposure and assignment risk quickly. Overnight market orders also face opening gaps and spreads. OIC: gamma.

Dated Greek availability is reported with the current market evidence.

Why covered-call constraints can leave shares uncovered

The 1%-above-basis floor expresses a willingness to sell the stock above its recorded purchase cost; the 2%-above-close floor preserves some current upside. Both are enforced, not preferences that the selector may trade away for premium. After a large decline, the basis floor may sit far above spot, leaving little premium or no qualifying observed call. The rule can prolong stock exposure; it does not make the combined position profitable.

Calls are evaluated only against confirmed, unencumbered shares. Existing short calls and pending orders prevent double-selling that coverage. Premium can offset part of a decline, but the call limits gains if the stock rallies; it is not a hedge against a large stock loss. OIC: covered calls.

What counts as a usable contract?

LSEG discovery supplies the current observed candidates; it is not treated as a complete historical chain. Ordinary OPRA RICs are cross-checked against strike, type, expiry and underlying metadata. Standard, American, USD and 100-share lot metadata must be present. Adjusted/mini or conflicting descriptions are excluded. No synthetic strike grid is used.

Historical DELTA_EXCH must carry the signal-session date. An undated Option Watch delta is not relabeled as historical. Observed candidate coverage and contract exclusions appear in the diagnostics.

Exposure = stock market value + short-put strike obligations, including pending puts. Covered calls do not add a second stock exposure.

Free cash = cash − existing and proposed put collateral. Expected premiums cannot fund the trade. When several puts qualify, allocate in ascending HV20 order (ticker breaks ties).

If existing holdings already breach a limit, no new orders are added. This version reports the breach; it does not force a liquidation.

The 30% single-name ceiling limits one issuer's claim on equity; the 70% total cap and 30% free-cash floor retain room for obligations and adverse marks. The cash test matters independently of exposure because stock is not immediately available cash. One contract per name further limits scale, so a fully eligible portfolio can still use substantially less than the 70% ceiling. The $40,000 starting value is an explicit course proposal assumption, not actual Sanji equity.

These constraints reduce concentration and funding pressure, not equity risk. A funded put can still require buying 100 shares well above their market value; reserved collateral cannot also be described as idle cash. Waiting with uncommitted cash may soften exposure during declines, but forgoes stock appreciation or other investment income. Expected option premiums cannot fund new orders, and no leverage is used to offset that opportunity cost. OIC: cash-secured puts.

10 / CAPITAL EFFICIENCY / MEASUREMENT CONVENTIONS

Capital Utilization and Return Measurement

Account return measures the change in the owner's full portfolio, including cash. Return on deployed capital asks how much net strategy profit was earned relative to the capital actually committed. Reporting both avoids praising a low-volatility account merely because much of it remained in cash, or hiding that cash's effect on total wealth.

Account Return = Net Portfolio P&L / Starting Portfolio Equity

Return on Deployed Capital = Net Strategy P&L / Average Deployed Capital

Capital Utilization = Deployed Capital / Portfolio Equity

The proposed convention uses each completed session's stock market value plus gross strike × 100 for filled short puts; a covered call adds no second stock amount. Average deployed capital will be the arithmetic mean across every trading session in the evaluation period, including cash-only days. Pending puts are also reserved by the planner, but their unfilled reservations will be disclosed separately from capital in executed positions. A mark-based denominator can fall when stocks lose value, so it is not a leverage-adjusted performance measure and must be read alongside account return and drawdown.

Evaluation will run between verified opening and closing account snapshots with complete intervening records, not convenient endpoints chosen after seeing returns. Net P&L will include realized and unrealized stock/option gains and losses, dividends and reported cash interest, less actual commissions and fees. Premium receipts are not profit while a short option liability remains. External deposits and withdrawals must be reconciled; the simple account-return formula assumes none, otherwise a flow-adjusted return is required. The capital-return ratio is descriptive and non-annualized, not a time-weighted return or Sharpe.

The example illustrates a capital stock at one date, not investment performance.

Actual account return, return on deployed capital and time-average utilization remain unmeasured until Sanji supplies the book and history. A single starting balance, a candidate option or three valid JSON proposals cannot supply their missing numerators or denominators.

What these decisions establish

The screen makes the willingness to take new bullish exposure conditional on observable inputs. It can reject a stock for weak trend alignment even when its recent volatility is acceptable. Refusing an order when its collateral or prior obligations cannot be checked is a separate control: it prevents an unverified obligation rather than predicting a loss. Neither kind of refusal tells us what an executed trade would have earned.

The present evidence supports a working decision policy and an auditable separation of inputs, filters and proposals. It does not test the hypothesis that premium collection improves risk-adjusted returns. That requires actual executions or a separately specified, point-in-time simulation, with the losses and missed opportunities included.

12 / EVALUATION & LIMITATIONS

Performance Evaluation Pending Sanji Data

Portfolio performance has not yet been calculated because verified Sanji execution and account NAV histories are unavailable. The current analysis concerns real market signals, risk checks and separately labeled hypothetical books. This is a missing evidence dependency, not an unexplained calculation error or a zero return.

The cost of conservative rules

Fewer qualifying entries mean fewer chances to collect premium, while cash can miss a rising market. A volatility cap may exclude precisely the contracts offering the largest premiums; a trend or drop filter may postpone entry until a rebound is already underway. The opposite possibility is that avoiding some entries reduces large losses. Today's rejections establish neither result. Cash drag and avoided losses require an executed or consistently simulated alternative path, not hindsight about rejected stocks.

After assignment, the risk changes

A put assignment exchanges reserved cash for 100 shares at the strike. The obligation becomes direct stock exposure; it is not a new profit, and loss can already exist at the assignment price. Subsequent covered calls exchange some upside for premium. These shares remain exposed when no call satisfies the strike floors, and the new-put filters do not trigger an automatic sale of existing stock. There are no stop-losses or rolls in this policy.

Sanji must confirm assignment and available shares before the planner can write a call. American-option exercise can occur before expiry, and dividend-related call assignment can alter both holdings and dividend entitlement. A simulator that simply assumes all options expire on Friday would miss those account transitions. The current planner delegates them to the authoritative book; the hypothetical inventory is not evidence that any assignment occurred.

What a valid performance record would contain

Actual fills, realized premium receipts, net P&L, account return, return on deployed capital, Sharpe, maximum drawdown and assignment frequency are all pending. A current account snapshot alone would not recover that history. Required records include fill IDs, times, quantities and prices; costs; dividends and cash flows; option resolutions; and daily reconciled cash, holdings, marks and NAV. Earlier single-stock assignments are not performance evidence for this four-stock policy.

The planned daily Sharpe uses genuine adjacent-session, flow-adjusted account returns, sample standard deviation and √252 annualization. A contemporaneous short-term Treasury risk-free series, dated without look-ahead and converted to daily units, will be specified before evaluating performance; 0% is only a separately labeled sensitivity comparison, not the default claim of excess return. Actual credited cash interest belongs in P&L. A simulated cash-yield assumption must state its source and accrual rule and be compared with a zero-interest sensitivity case. Neither interest series nor performance is calculated here. Maximum drawdown will use the entire flow-adjusted wealth path relative to its running peak, including opening equity (the NAV path when there are no external flows). Missing marks remain missing: full-period statistics will be withheld rather than bridging returns, interpolating NAV or substituting zero. Any partial-sample statistic would have its own dates and explicit limitations.

A comparison to run, not a result already obtained

The proposed baseline is a weekly Wheel on the same four names, retaining cash security, account limits and contract-selection rules but omitting the three technical entry screens. Compare it with this risk-managed version using identical market periods, initial equity, observed contract access, transaction costs, assignment treatment and execution assumptions. This isolates the screens without granting either strategy leverage or better fills. It is a proposed research protocol; the comparison has not run.

Sanji history for only the selected strategy cannot reveal fills for the baseline's counterfactual trades. Those would need a separately labeled, point-in-time simulation or parallel course evaluation. Compare net account and deployed-capital returns, utilization, drawdown, Sharpe and assignment frequency together. Keep the rules fixed, then test sensitivity over other periods without selecting parameters on the evaluation sample.

What remains uncertain — and how to resolve it

The IV–RV thesis is untested. This project has no paired, reliable entry-date implied volatility and subsequent realized-volatility sample for the historical option trades. HV20 is backward-looking stock volatility, not IV. Neither the eligibility analysis nor a put proposal establishes that an implied-volatility premium was earned.

Current LSEG discovery is an observed candidate set, not a complete historical chain. A fallback is reproducible within that set but can select differently as coverage changes. Missing dated Greeks prevent a claim that demonstrated proposals are 25-delta trades. A replay needs archived point-in-time chains and contract exclusions, so later discoveries cannot establish earlier availability. The historical section tests variation in eligibility across periods, not regime-specific investment returns, correlation protection or the performance of alternative parameter choices.

For authoritative account operation, the professor must supply the native Sanji account schema, actual cash/equity and complete positions, plus update-complete timing. Bases, marks and resolution IDs are needed when the corresponding positions or orders exist; a flat confirmed book has no such positions to value. For execution, course enrollment and contract-open coverage for the four verified conids remain unconfirmed. Resolver activation is needed to observe course executions, not to validate a proposal's JSON. Course mode already supports hypothetical cash-only proposals without these official records. No earnings-calendar entitlement or alternative event feed is required.

These records can feed a separate, read-only performance report alongside the existing market, decision and order artifacts. That preserves the planner and page layout while adding auditable account evidence; no production balance or fill needs to be invented to make the current website look complete.

Earnings-event risk. The strategy does not screen around earnings announcements and may therefore be exposed to earnings-related price gaps. Trend, volatility and cash-reserve checks do not eliminate this risk.

Equity risk. Four stocks can decline together. Put assignment can create losses larger than premium income; calls cap upside and do not remove downside.

Execution risk. Short-dated gamma, overnight gaps, early assignment, dividends, spreads, fees and market-order slippage matter. No fills or fees are invented by the nightly planner.

Data risk. Observed option coverage may be incomplete. Greeks can be missing or revised. A fallback changes selection—not the facts—and historical replay would need point-in-time snapshots.
